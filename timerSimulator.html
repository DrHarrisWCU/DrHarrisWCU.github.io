What you're looking at: the top wave is a sawtooth — a counter climbing steadily and snapping back to zero once it hits the limit you set, the way a microcontroller's internal timer register counts on repeat. The bottom wave, PWM, comes from comparing that counter against a threshold: the signal is on while the count is below it and off once it passes (or the reverse, if inverted). How much of each cycle is spent "on" is the duty cycle — it's how a simple on/off signal like this can dim an LED or control a motor's speed.
